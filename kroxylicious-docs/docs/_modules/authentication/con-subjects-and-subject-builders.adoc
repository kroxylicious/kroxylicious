= Understanding subjects and subject builders

[role="_abstract"]
When the proxy authenticates a client, it constructs an _authenticated subject_ that represents the client's identity.
This subject is made available to filters in the proxy chain so that they can make decisions based on who the client is.

== Subjects and principals

A subject contains a set of _principals_.
Each principal is a named identifier that represents one aspect of the client's identity.
For example, a principal might represent a user name, an email address, or a group membership.

A subject may contain at most one `User` principal, which represents the primary identity of the client.

If no authentication is configured, or if authentication has not yet completed, the subject is _anonymous_ — it contains no principals.

== Subject builders

Subject builders are responsible for constructing the authenticated subject from information obtained during authentication.
The proxy provides two types of subject builder, corresponding to the two authentication mechanisms:

Transport subject builder::
Builds a subject from information in the client's TLS certificate.
The transport subject builder runs after the TLS handshake completes and has access to the client certificate's distinguished name (DN) and subject alternative names (SANs).
+
The transport subject builder is configured on the virtual cluster.
See xref:con-configuring-vc-transport-subject-builder-{context}[] for configuration details.

SASL subject builder::
Builds a subject from information obtained during a successful SASL authentication exchange, such as the SASL authorization ID.
+
The SASL subject builder is configured on the SASL filter (for example, the `SaslInspection` filter).
See xref:con-configuring-sasl-subject-builder-{context}[] for configuration details.

Both subject builders use a pluggable architecture.
The default implementations extract principals from certificate fields or SASL authorization IDs using configurable mapping rules.
Custom implementations can augment the subject with information looked up from external trusted systems, such as a directory service (for example, ActiveDirectory or LDAP), to add additional principals representing roles or group memberships.

== Subject lifecycle

The authenticated subject evolves through the connection lifecycle:

. *TLS handshake* — If a transport subject builder is configured and the client presents a TLS certificate, the builder creates the initial subject.
  If no transport subject builder is configured, or if TLS client authentication is not enabled, the subject starts as anonymous.

. *SASL authentication* — If a SASL-aware filter (such as the `SaslInspection` filter) is configured and the client authenticates successfully via SASL, the SASL subject builder creates a new subject that _replaces_ the transport subject.

. *Re-authentication* — Kafka supports re-authentication (https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/KAFKA/KIP-368%3A+Allow+SASL+Connections+to+Periodically+Re-Authenticate[KIP-368]).
  When a client re-authenticates, the SASL subject builder creates a new subject that replaces the previous one.

. *Authentication failure* — If SASL authentication fails, the subject reverts to anonymous.
